Overview

Instant Flow Q-Learning (InsQL) overview. (a) Standard flow-matching (diffusion) policies learn an instantaneous marginal velocity field \(v(a_t,t,s)\) and generate actions through multi-step integration. (b) InsQL instead learns an average-velocity field \(u(a_t,n,t,s)\) over finite intervals, enabling single-step action generation by directly mapping noise to actions. (c) Velocity composition: long-range average velocities are recursively composed from shorter-range velocities; in the infinitesimal limit, this recovers the marginal velocity of flow matching. (d) InsQL integrates the one-step policy into a behavior-regularized actor–critic framework without backpropagation through time, with optional Q-guided candidate selection for test-time refinement.

Algorithm Overview

Three key components of InsQL:

  • 1Compositional Average-Velocity Learning

    Standard flow matching learns instantaneous velocity fields and requires multiple integration steps to generate actions. InsQL instead learns average velocities over finite intervals, whose compositional property allows long-range velocities to be computed from shorter-range velocities. This enables a direct one-step mapping from noise to actions.

  • 2Flow-Consistent Policy Learning

    To ensure the learned average-velocity field follows the underlying flow dynamics, InsQL anchors the model to the marginal velocity at small intervals. These constraints are incorporated directly into the behavior cloning objective, allowing the policy to consistently learn average velocities across different ranges without auxiliary models, distillation, or multi-stage training.

  • 3Single-Step Q-Learning with Q-Guided Selection

    InsQL integrates the one-step generative policy into a behavior-regularized, DDPG-style actor–critic framework without backpropagation through time. At inference, multiple candidate actions can be generated in a single forward pass and refined through Q-guided candidate selection, improving action quality without iterative denoising.

Experimental Results

Q1: Can InsQL outperform both diffusion-based and existing one-step policies?

Answer: Yes. InsQL consistently outperforms both multi-step diffusion policies and existing one-step generative policies across a range of offline reinforcement learning benchmarks.

On MuJoCo locomotion tasks, InsQL achieves an average score of 92.1, surpassing DQL (89.0) and FQL (79.2). InsQL also achieves strong performance on challenging long-horizon domains, including AntMaze

Figure: InsQL consistently outperforms both diffusion-based and one-step baselines across diverse offline reinforcement learning benchmarks.

Q2: Can one-step generation overcome the diffusion-policy bottleneck?

Answer: Yes. Existing approaches often suffer a significant performance drop when diffusion sampling is reduced to a single step. InsQL avoids this degradation while achieving performance comparable to or better than multi-step diffusion policies.

By learning compositional average velocities instead of relying on iterative denoising, InsQL preserves the expressiveness of generative policies while enabling direct noise-to-action generation in a single forward pass.

Q3: Does InsQL improve practical efficiency?

Answer: Yes. InsQL significantly reduces both training and inference costs.

InsQL eliminates iterative denoising and backpropagation through time, allowing actions to be generated in a single forward pass. Compared with DQL, InsQL achieves up to 3.6× faster inference while substantially reducing training time and maintaining strong policy performance.

Figure: InsQL delivers strong performance while substantially improving both training and inference efficiency.

Q4: How many Q-guided candidates are needed to improve performance?

Answer: Around 5 candidates provide a significant performance improvement compared with using no Q-guided selection.

At inference time, InsQL can sample multiple candidate actions in a single forward pass and select the action with the highest predicted Q-value. This provides an effective way to improve action quality without introducing iterative denoising.
